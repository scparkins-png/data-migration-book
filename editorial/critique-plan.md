# Critical Edit — Create Your Plan

27 October 2026 · branch `critical-edit-plan` · same pattern as Chapters 1–7

## Verdict

This is a short chapter and its message is right: plan first, and set your own timeline. These parts carry it:
- the honest "I know, I have done exactly that";
- the five Key things;
- the "two to three months, max" story;
- the job / Works Order / Manufacturing Order example.

The weaknesses are mostly in the shape, because this chapter was written before the step-chapter pattern was settled:
- **A different opener.** There are horizontal rules between every section, "In this chapter you will…" is bold text rather than a heading, and there's a figure where the other chapters have the progress strip.
- **One sentence or four?** "In this chapter you will…" promises a "one-sentence migration aim" covering what, why, success *and* scope. That can't fit in one sentence, and the exercise has five parts.
- **The *Know the Business* section** is bold text rather than a heading (C13). Its five paragraphs repeat Appendix E almost word for word, and it still cites the Flow7 Data Mapping workbook (M11).
- **From the Field** opens with an orphaned bold line and gives its advice before its story.
- **Exercises** still have write-in boxes and a blank risk table. Exercise 4 is "open the spreadsheet", which is an instruction, not practice.
- Copy-edit items C10–C12 ("divert your resources", the missing comma, the double space).

Words: about 1,890 → 1,650.

## Proposals

**P1 — Standard opener.** Subtitle: *"The Foundation — before the seven steps of the Flow7 Migration System"*. The horizontal rules go. The Flow7 figure is replaced by the progress strip, marked `{{progress: foundation}}` (no step filled), so this chapter looks like the first stop on the same road. The full Flow7 map (fig-plan-01) moves to the front of the book, as planned in the signposting system.

**P2 — The aim and the statement, separated.** The *aim* is one sentence: what you are moving and why. The *Outline Migration Statement* is that aim plus success, scope and sign-off. "In this chapter you will…", "What You Are Actually Doing", Key thing 1 and Exercise 2 now all say the same thing, and the exercise tells the reader that questions 1 and 2 together are the aim.

**P3 — "What you'll need" gains** "an hour with the person who will sign the plan off". The planning chapter is the one where their time matters most.

**P4 — Key thing 4 fixed** (C12). "Think through what could go wrong … and divert your resources" becomes "What could go wrong for your business, and how would you respond?" "More on this later" is cut from Why This Matters, because nothing later picks it up.

**P5 — *Know the Business — and Its Language* becomes a proper heading** (C13), placed after Key things so the chapter's own work comes first. It's cut from five paragraphs to three, plus two boxes:
- **Watch out:** the OPTEDIN / Email Opt Out example, now introduced as "one of my own mapping projects" (closes M11 for this chapter; Appendix E still has its citation to fix);
- **Key idea:** "Carry this as a lens, not a step."

The list of false friends and domains is left to Appendix E. The step-by-step "what each step teaches you" walk-through is cut, because *Know the Business* has that as its table.

**P6 — From the Field reordered.** It now has a headline (*whose timeline?*), then the story, then the advice that follows from it. The orphaned bold line becomes the closing sentence.

**P7 — Your Turn: four exercises → three.** Exercise 2 becomes five numbered questions, with Worksheet P.2. Exercise 3 becomes one paragraph, with Worksheet P.3. Exercise 4 ("open the Project Plan Checklist") moves into the *Your template* box, where it belongs. The gate still checks it.

**P8 — Gate and ending.** The duplicate "Stop/Go Gate" heading goes. The bold on every gate row goes (no other chapter has it). The chapter ends with **Next:** Chapter 1.
