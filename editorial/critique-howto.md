# Critical Edit — How to Use This Handbook

27 October 2026 · branch `critical-edit-howto`

## Verdict

The voice is right, and so is most of the content: "the method doing its job", "they know where the bodies are buried", "that folder *is* your migration". The problem is that it describes the book as it was *before* the decisions we've made since:
- **It tells readers to write in the book** ("fill the boxes in pen", "a blank handbook helped no one"). Under the hybrid decision, the answer space is in the worksheet pack (M9).
- **The chapter pattern is out of date.** "In this chapter" is listed before "Where you are" — every chapter does the reverse (D6/X13). It leaves out "What you'll need", the worked examples and the template box, and still promises "an exercise to do on the page".
- **It never explains the three parts,** that Chapter N is Step N, or Appendix E.
- **No key to the boxes.** The signposting system agreed that How to Use carries the key, so readers learn the box types once.
- **The illustrative scenario isn't mentioned.** The kitchenware wholesaler now appears in Chapters 1, 4 and 7, and readers should know it's invented before they meet it.
- Copy-edit items C8 ("50,000 thousand records") and C9 (double space).

This is the one chapter that grows: about 870 → 1,150 words. The extra length is the box key and the parts list — both things readers will use rather than read once.

## Proposals

**P1 — The three parts, explained up front.** A short list: Part One (this page and Create Your Plan), Part Two (Steps 1–7, where Chapter N is Step N), Part Three (Know the Business). Appendix E is named as the reference to keep open.

**P2 — *Tracking back* named.** The existing "nip back and fix something earlier" paragraph now tells readers there's a box for exactly that.

**P3 — The chapter pattern, corrected and grouped** (closes D6/X13). It matches the chapters exactly and is grouped into three: **the opener** (where you are, what you'll do, what you'll need), **the work** (why, what, key things, from the field, checklist, your turn) and **the proof** (the gate). Grouping makes ten items easy to take in. "Your checklist" now says the full list lives in the Excel file.

**P4 — A box key: six boxes, one job each.** Key idea, Watch out, Tracking back, Worked example, Your template, Know the business. I've listed only the ones the manuscript actually uses; the design stage will give each its look.

**P5 — The heavy lifting, rewritten around the companion files** (closes M9 for this chapter). Three named kinds of file, all at stuartparkins.com: the worksheet pack (numbered to match the exercises), the step templates, and the Project Plan Checklist. Your "that folder *is* your migration" line is kept.

**P6 — The examples paragraph names the illustrative scenario,** says it's invented, and keeps your "the people may be part-fiction; the problems never are".

**P7 — "How to actually work it" → "How to work it".** "Write in it" becomes "Work it, don't just read it". The other three points are unchanged.

## Not changed

- The opening and closing lines (apart from "work *in*" → "work *through*").
- "Bring your people with you" (the "particular person in the room" line slightly tightened).
