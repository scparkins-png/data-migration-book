# Critical Edit — Chapter 1: Know Your Data Sources (pilot)

25 October 2026 · branch `critical-edit-ch1`

## Verdict

The substance is strong. The purpose is clear, the examples are concrete and memorable — the receptionist's "do not call" list, "Dynamics NAV 2013, customised" — and the case study lands its point. It's the best example in the book of the source problem that people miss.

What gets in the way is **packaging**:
- the opening says the same thing three times;
- there are 20 checklist items and an 8-item gate;
- the ending has four separate closings.

A first-time reader has to work too hard to find the five things they actually need to do. The proposals below keep every idea and cut about a quarter of the words: 2,560 → about 1,900.

## Proposals

Each one: what changes, and why. The branch has the chapter with all of them applied, so you can read it whole. Reply with the numbers you want changed or dropped.

**P1 — One opener instead of three.**
- *Before:* an italic intro paragraph, then the bold quote between two rules, then "Where You Are" — all saying "first, find your data". "Where does all that data actually live?" appears three times before the work starts.
- *After:* your quote as the hook, then "Where You Are in Flow7" in two sentences. The best line from the italic intro is kept: "Don't skip it because you think you already know where your data lives."
- *Why:* this becomes the fixed opener for every step chapter.

**P2 — "What you'll need" lists what's actually needed.**
- *Before:* "What you'll need from earlier chapters" lists two things that aren't from earlier chapters.
- *After:* retitled "What you'll need". It adds the Outline Migration Statement and readiness assessment from *Create Your Plan*, so the reader sees the link back.

**P3 — Why This Matters leads with the win.**
- *Before:* it restates the planning chapter, then a hard-to-read sentence about extra sources.
- *After:* three plain sentences on what a complete inventory gives you in Steps 2, 3 and 5, and what a missed source costs.
- The extra-sources point becomes the book's first **Tracking back** box: "If an extra source turns up later — and it can — come back, add it to the inventory and carry on. That is the method working, not failing."

**P4 — The definition comes first.**
- *Before:* "What counts as a data source?" sits at the end of the section, as one run-on line with dashes.
- *After:* it moves to the start of "What You Are Actually Doing", as a proper list. "Emails" gets its own line.
- *Why:* a reader needs the definition before the method.

**P5 — Four things, not "three layers".**
- *Before:* "three layers of information", followed by four items.
- *After:* "four things", in plain wording, each mapped to its template sheet.
- The second "see Know the Business" cross-reference is shortened, because the new signpost at the end of the chapter now does that job.

**P6 — Checklist: 20 items → 6.**
- *Before:* sections 1A, 1B and 1C list 20 "Record the…" items. Most are just the column headings of the Excel sheet.
- *After:* six essential actions, each tagged 1A, 1B or 1C. A line says the full list, with status, owner and date columns, is in the Excel checklist.
- *Note:* this replaces the Pass 2 rule that checklist items are copied word for word from the Flow7 checklist. The Excel file keeps the full wording; the book shows a summary. **Please confirm.**

**P7 — Your Turn: three exercises → two.**
- *Before:* Exercise 1.2, the mini-inventory, is a smaller version of the Inventory sheet. It repeats what the checklist and template already do.
- *After:* keep the five-minute brainstorm (1.1) and the export reality check (now 1.2), which are the two that make a reader think. **Your call** — say if you'd rather keep all three.

**P8 — One standard "Your template" box.**
- *Before:* a heading ("Now Go to the Full Template") with a bullet list.
- *After:* a short box naming the three Step 1 sheets, the Project Plan Checklist and one web address. Every chapter will use this same box.

**P9 — Gate: 8 items → 6.**
- *Before:* separate rows for "asked frontline staff" and "listed every source", and for "owner" and "priority".
- *After:* those pairs are merged into one row each. Nothing is lost.

**P10 — One ending, not four.**
- *Before:* "What you have achieved", then the gate, then a paragraph after the gate, then a final line ("…how good that data is?") that repeats it, then the signpost.
- *After:* "What you have achieved", then the gate with one short warning line, then a one-line **Next** pointer, then the *Know the business* signpost. Every chapter will end the same way.

**P11 — Small fixes made while rewriting.**
- *VPN bullet:* the long sentence is split. "Replicate live data" is replaced with what Chapter 3 says actually happened: a local copy of the data to work from.
- The typos in the old edit-log items C14–C19 are gone with the rewrite.

## Not changed

- **Key things to get right:** the four disciplines stand as written. They are the heart of the chapter.
- **From the Field:** the story and your voice ("Wrong on all counts", "have a look") are kept.
- **The worked-example placeholder:** still waiting for real data (see Q3).
