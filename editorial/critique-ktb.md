# Critical Edit — Know the Business

27 October 2026 · branch `critical-edit-ktb` · the last chapter in the critical edit

## Verdict

The idea is the book's best: a migration's quiet second product is a record of how the business really works. The walk-back table and "the data moved; the knowledge stayed" are excellent. Three things hold the chapter back:

- **No payoff for the signposts.** Every step chapter ends by asking readers to note what they learned "for the closing chapter" — and this chapter never asks for those notes. There is nothing to *do* here.
- **The old reference style.** Two numbered references to your own case study, a third-person "documented by Stuart Parkins" line (D7), and an italic note about Appendix E that already appears in Appendix E.
- **A small inconsistency.** It says the knowledge was "trapped in one person's head"; the Foreword says "a couple of employees' memories". Readers notice.

Words: about 1,190 → 1,200 (cuts and the new exercise roughly balance).

## Proposals

**P1 — Opener.** The hook becomes the bold promise line, in the same place as the other chapters. Where You Are drops one sentence and points to the section by its real heading in *Create Your Plan*.

**P2 — A Your Turn exercise and Worksheet KB.1: "Hand the knowledge on".** Four questions:
- what did each step teach you?
- where does that knowledge live now? ("still in someone's head" is an allowed answer);
- who owns the Terminology Translator?
- who needs to know?

A *Key idea* box follows: anything still in someone's head is your first job after the migration. This closes the loop on the seven *Know the business* signposts. The worksheet pack goes from 14 to 15.

**P3 — The translator gets an owner.** "Keep it with the project handover" becomes "keep it with the handover, and give it an owner", which ties into question 3.

**P4 — From the Field, in your voice.** It gets the standard headline, drops "documented by Stuart Parkins" (D7) and says "one or two people's heads", to match the Foreword. It now names the 1,500+ assembly instructions, which makes the knowledge risk concrete.

**P5 — References removed** (closes X15). Both cite your own case study, which *About the Sources* covers. The italic Appendix E note goes too, because Appendix E already says the same thing.

**P6 — Small wording.** "Quietly tolerated" becomes "quietly put up with". "Survives a system change" becomes "comes through a system change stronger". "Fall out of alignment" becomes "fall out of step". "As-is" becomes "as they stand".

## Not changed

- The walk-back table (shortened wording only).
- The two kinds of knowledge.
- "Looking Further", moved here in the Chapter 7 edit, still closes the book.
