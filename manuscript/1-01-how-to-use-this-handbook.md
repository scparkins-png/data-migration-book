# How to Use This Handbook

This is a handbook you work *in*, not just a book you simply read. It's built to sit on your desk through a live migration — annotated, dog-eared, and a little coffee-stained by the end. Here's how to get the most out of it.

## Read the shape first, then work the steps

The heart of the handbook is the **Flow7 Migration System** — seven ordered steps that take you from "where does our data actually live?" all the way to a confirmed go-live. Before those, a short chapter called **Create Your Plan** sets up the planning that makes everything else possible.

Work the steps in order. Each one builds on the one before: you can't decide what your single version of the truth is (Step 3) until you know what data you have and what condition it's in (Steps 1 and 2). That said, a real migration is rarely a tidy straight line. You'll often reach a later step and realise you need to nip back and fix something earlier — a source you missed, a process nobody mentioned. That's not a failure; it's the method doing its job. Move forward when you're ready, back when you need to.

## Every chapter follows the same rhythm

Once you know the pattern, you'll navigate any chapter at a glance. Each step-chapter is built the same way:

- **In this chapter you will…** — the handful of things you'll have done by the end.
- **Where you are in Flow7** — a quick reminder of how this step connects to the ones either side of it.
- **Why this matters** — the win this step secures, in plain terms.
- **What you're actually doing** — the work itself, explained without jargon.
- **Key things to get right** — the specific traps this step helps you avoid.
- **From the field** — what happened at this step on the real Sage 50 to Sage X3 migration that runs through the book.
- **Your checklist** — the actions for this step, the same list I'd work through on a live project.
- **Your turn** — a short exercise to do on the page, then a pointer to the tool or template for the heavy lifting.
- **You're ready to move on when…** — a clear STOP or GO  gate: the conditions that mean this step is genuinely finished.

Don't skip the gate. It's the single most useful line in each chapter. If you can't honestly tick everything in "ready to move on when…", you're not ready — and moving on anyway is how migrations come unstuck.

## The thinking happens here; the heavy lifting happens in the tools

You'll do plenty of thinking right on these pages — short exercises, decision prompts, boxes to fill in. But some of the real work is far too big for paper. Nobody maps four hundred fields or tests 50,000 thousand records on a printed line. So wherever a job needs a proper tool, I point you to one: a spreadsheet, a template, an approach built for exactly that task. This is a handbook, not a technical manual — it tells you *what* to do and *why*, and hands you off to the right tool for the *how*. Keep a folder (digital or physical) alongside the book for those working files. By the end, that folder *is* your migration.

## Bring your people with you

You'll notice a theme from the very first step: a migration is only partly about data. It's just as much about the people who hold knowledge in their heads, the processes nobody wrote down, and the language your business uses that the new system won't recognise. So don't do this alone in a quiet room. The exercises work best when you pull in the people who actually use the data every day — they know where the bodies are buried. Where a chapter benefits from a particular person in the room, I'll say so.

## A note on the examples

One real migration threads through the whole book — a **Sage 50 to Sage X3** move — so you follow a single project from start to finish rather than a scatter of disconnected anecdotes. Alongside it, I use shorter examples from other work I've done to either show the same lesson in a different setting or help explain. Where an example involves a real client, I've changed the details and sometimes built a composite character, so the lesson stands without exposing anyone's business. Anything illustrative is clearly labelled as such. The people may occasionally be part-fiction; the problems never are.

## How to actually work it

If you take nothing else from this page, take this:

- **Write in it.** Underline, argue in the margins, fill the boxes in pen. A blank handbook helped no one.
- **Go in order, but don't be a slave to it.** Forward by default; back when the work tells you to.
- **Be honest at the STOP/GO gates.** They only protect you if you tell them the truth.
- **Don't rush the early steps.** The temptation is always to start moving data. The discipline is to understand it first. That discipline is the whole game.
