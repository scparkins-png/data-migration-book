# How to Use This Handbook

This is a handbook you work *through*, not just a book you read. It's built to sit on your desk during a live migration — open at the step you're on, dog-eared, and a little coffee-stained by the end. Here's how to get the most out of it.

## Read the shape first, then work the steps

The book is in three parts:

- **Part One — Getting Started.** This page, then **Create Your Plan**: the planning that makes everything else possible.
- **Part Two — The Flow7 Data Migration System.** Seven ordered steps, one chapter each, taking you from "where does our data actually live?" to a confirmed go-live. Chapter 1 is Step 1, Chapter 7 is Step 7.
- **Part Three — After the Move.** **Know the Business**, where you pull together what the project taught you about how the business works and the words it uses.

At the back, **Appendix A — The Terminology Translator** is the reference to keep open from planning to go-live, and **Appendix B** lists every Flow7 download, step by step.

Work the steps in order. Each one builds on the one before: you can't decide on your single version of the truth (Step 3) until you know what data you have and what condition it's in (Steps 1 and 2). That said, a real migration is rarely a tidy straight line. You'll often reach a later step and realise you need to nip back and fix something earlier — a source you missed, a process nobody mentioned. That's not a failure; it's the method doing its job. Whenever it happens, a **Tracking back** box tells you which step to return to. Move forward when you're ready, back when you need to.

## Every step chapter follows the same pattern

Once you know the pattern, you'll find your way round any chapter at a glance. Each one has three parts.

**The opener — where you are and what's ahead**
- **Where you are in Flow7** — a "you are here" strip and a sentence or two on what this step does.
- **In this chapter you will…** — the handful of things you'll have done by the end.
- **What you'll need** — what to bring from earlier steps, by name.

**The work — what to do and why**
- **Why this matters** — the win this step secures, in plain terms.
- **What you're actually doing** — the work itself, explained without jargon.
- **Key things to get right** — the traps this step helps you avoid.
- **From the field** — what happened at this step on the real Sage 50 to Sage X3 migration.
- **Your checklist** — the essential tasks. The full list, with owners and dates, is in the Excel Project Plan Checklist.
- **Your turn** — a short exercise to practise the step on your own data.

**The proof — are you really done?**
- **You're ready to move on when…** — the STOP/GO gate: the conditions that mean this step is genuinely finished.

Don't skip the gate. It's the most useful part of each chapter. If you can't honestly tick every box, you're not ready — and moving on anyway is how migrations come unstuck.

## The boxes

Six kinds of box appear throughout the book. Each one has one job:

| Box | What it's for |
|:-|:-|
| 🔑 **Key idea** | The one thing to remember from this section |
| 👁 **Watch out** | A trap, or a legal point such as GDPR |
| ↩ **Tracking back** | You've found a gap — here's the earlier step to go back to |
| 📋 **Worked example** | A filled-in template, so you can see what "good" looks like |
| 📄 **Your template** | Which file to open, and where to get it |

## The thinking happens here; the heavy lifting happens in the tools

You'll do plenty of thinking with this book open — short exercises and decision prompts in every chapter. But much of the real work is far too big for a page. Nobody maps four hundred fields or tests 50,000 records on a printed line. So wherever a job needs a proper tool, I point you to one, in a **Your template** box.

There are three kinds of companion file, all at **stuartparkins.com**:

- **The worksheet pack** — the answer space for every *Your turn* exercise, numbered to match (Exercise 2.1 → Worksheet 2.1). Print it, or fill it in on screen.
- **The Flow7 step templates** — one per step, for the real work: source inventories, mapping sheets, test scripts.
- **The Flow7 Data Migration Project Plan Checklist** — the Excel file that tracks every task, owner and date across all seven steps.

If you prefer to work in a spreadsheet, **the Flow7 Migration Workbook** puts all the step templates into one Excel file.

This is a handbook, not a technical manual. It tells you *what* to do and *why*, and hands you to the right tool for the *how*. Keep a folder — digital or physical — for your worksheets and working files. By the end, that folder *is* your migration.

## Bring your people with you

You'll notice a theme from the very first step: a migration is only partly about data. It's just as much about the people who hold knowledge in their heads, the processes nobody wrote down, and the language your business uses that the new system won't recognise. So don't do this alone in a quiet room. The exercises work best when you pull in the people who use the data every day — they know where the bodies are buried. Where a chapter needs a particular person in the room, I'll say so.

## A note on the examples

One real migration runs through the whole book — a **Sage 50 to Sage X3** move for a manufacturer — so you follow a single project from start to finish, rather than a scatter of disconnected anecdotes. Where an example involves a real client, I've changed the details, and sometimes built a composite character, so the lesson stands without exposing anyone's business.

Alongside it, a smaller **illustrative scenario** — a kitchenware wholesaler moving to a new cloud ERP — appears in the worked examples, so you can see the templates filled in. That business is invented, and every illustrative example is labelled as such. The people may be part-fiction; the problems never are.

## How to work it

If you take nothing else from this page, take this:

- **Work it, don't just read it.** Do the exercises, fill in the worksheets, argue in the margins.
- **Go in order, but don't be a slave to it.** Forward by default; back when the work tells you to.
- **Be honest at the STOP/GO gates.** They only protect you if you tell them the truth.
- **Don't rush the early steps.** The temptation is always to start moving data. The discipline is to understand it first. That discipline is the whole game.
